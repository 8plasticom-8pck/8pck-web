What Buyers Get Wrong

Cap Specified After the Mould

A 38-400 cap will not fit a 38-410 neck, and both exist on jugs.

Handle Placed for the Shelf

A jug that pours badly is returned whatever the price. Check the pour angle on a filled sample.

Label on a Handled Curve

Wrap labels work; silkscreen on the curved handle area prints poorly.

Handles Change the Mould

A jug is a bottle with a handle, and the handle is the mould. An offset neck leaves room for a full grip; a centred neck with a side handle needs a heavier wall at the joint. Both are blow-moulded in HDPE and both start at 1,000 pcs.

The common sizes are 1 gallon (3.8 L), half-gallon (1.9 L), 2-3 L detergent and 5 L jerry can. Necks run 38-400 for most, 63 mm for wide-mouth.

What Buyers Get Wrong

Two things: the cap is specified after the mould (a 38-400 cap will not fit a 38-410 neck, and both exist), and the handle is placed for the shelf rather than the pour. A jug that pours badly is returned regardless of price.

Decorating a Jug

Labels are the practical option; silkscreen on a curved handled surface is possible but the print area is small. Most brands use a wrap label plus a moulded-in volume mark.

Frequently Asked

What sizes of plastic jug do you make?

1 gallon (3.8 L), half-gallon (1.9 L), 2-3 L detergent and 5 L jerry cans, all HDPE blow-moulded.

Which neck do jugs use?

Mostly 38-400; wide-mouth versions use 63 mm.

Can a jug be decorated?

Wrap labels are standard. Silkscreen is possible on the flat panels but the printable area is small.

What is the MOQ?

1,000 pcs per colour for stock moulds; a new handle shape needs tooling.
