What Buyers Get Wrong

LDPE Where the Bottle Must Stand

Soft-wall LDPE collapses as it empties. For shelf presence, use HDPE.

Sauce Neck Too Narrow

38 mm is the filling-speed standard; a 24/410 sauce bottle slows the line and clogs the orifice.

No Valve in the Cap

Without a silicone valve, thick sauces drip after dispensing — the most common complaint.

Soft Wall or Firm Wall

LDPE is the soft squeeze: it collapses as it empties, which suits sauces, gels and anything dispensed by pressure. HDPE is firmer and springs back, which suits lotions and products where the bottle should keep its shape on a shelf.

The test is simple: fill it, squeeze it, and see whether it returns. A sauce bottle that does not return looks cheap; a lotion bottle that collapses looks broken.

Necks and Dispensing

Sauce bottles use a wide 38 mm neck for filling and a small orifice cap for dispensing. Dropper squeeze bottles use 18/410 with a tip insert. Cosmetic squeeze tubes and bottles sit in between at 24/410.

If the product is thick, a silicone valve in the cap gives a clean cut-off and stops the drip that customers complain about.

MOQ and Colour

1,000 pcs per colour on stock moulds, 25-35 days. LDPE takes colour well but shows flow lines on translucent shades; opaque white and black are the safest first order.

Frequently Asked

LDPE or HDPE for a squeeze bottle?

LDPE if the bottle should collapse as it empties (sauces, gels); HDPE if it should spring back and keep its shape (lotions).

What neck does a sauce squeeze bottle use?

Usually 38 mm for filling speed, with a small-orifice cap or a silicone valve for clean cut-off.

Can squeeze bottles be used for cosmetics?

Yes — 30-250 ml LDPE with 24/410 or 18/410 closures, common for cleansers and travel products.

What is the MOQ?

1,000 pcs per colour, 25-35 days after sample approval.
