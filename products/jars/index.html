Cosmetic Jars and Food Jars Are Different Products

A cosmetic jar is sold by fill weight (30 g, 50 g, 100 g) and judged by the weight in the hand and the sound of the lid. A food jar is sold by volume and judged by whether the seal holds and how fast it fills. They share a mould shop, not a specification.

Cosmetic jars are usually single-wall PET or double-wall PETG. Double wall costs more and feels expensive; it is also heavier to ship. Food jars are PET for cold fill and PP where the product goes in hot.

Sealing, and Where Jars Fail

For cosmetics, the thread and the liner decide whether the cream leaks into the cap. For food, an induction seal liner needs a flat land on the rim; not every jar design has one, and that is worth checking before you print the carton.

Hot fill above roughly 60 C distorts standard PET. For jam and sauces we quote PP, even though clear PET looks better on shelf.

MOQ and Colour

1,000 pcs per colour on existing moulds. Frosting is a surface treatment, not a colour, so it does not add a changeover; spray coat and metallising start at 3,000 pcs.
