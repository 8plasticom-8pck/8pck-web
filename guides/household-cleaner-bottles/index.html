Household Cleaner Bottles: Trigger Sprayers, Vents and the Chemistry Inside

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

A cleaner bottle is a chemical container before it is a brand object. The resin, the gasket and the vent decide whether it survives the product — and the trigger decides whether it survives the customer.

Household cleaners ship in 500–1,000 ml HDPE with a trigger sprayer, or in 750 ml–2 L with a cap for refill. The product is often alkaline or solvent-based, which rules out some resins and most low-cost gaskets.

This page covers the resin choice, the trigger, and the two failures that come back most often.

Resin Follows the Chemistry

Alkaline cleaners, bleach and solvents attack PET and many gaskets. HDPE handles most household chemistry; the gasket inside the trigger is the part that fails first.

For a fragrance-heavy or solvent product, the trigger's internal seal material has to be specified — not assumed.

HDPE

The default for alkaline and general cleaners, 500–1,000 ml.

PET

Only for mild, near-neutral formulas; clarity is the reason to choose it.

Two Failures That Come Back

The trigger leaks at the nozzle after a few hundred strokes: usually a worn piston seal or a formula that swells it. Test the trigger with the real liquid, not water.

The bottle sucks in as it empties: a non-vented cap on a tight bottle. A vented cap or a small air hole costs nothing at mould stage.

Refill and Concentrate Formats

Concentrates are moving into 250–500 ml bottles with a measuring cap, and refills into 1–2 L with a wide neck. The pack changes with the format, not the brand.

Questions We Get on This

What bottle do household cleaners use?

HDPE, 500–1,000 ml, with a 28/410 trigger or a 38 mm cap.

Can PET hold cleaner?

Only mild, near-neutral formulas; alkaline and solvent products need HDPE.

Why does my trigger leak?

The piston seal swells or wears with the formula. Test with the real liquid, not water.

Why does the bottle collapse?

No vent. Use a vented cap or add a small air hole at mould stage.
