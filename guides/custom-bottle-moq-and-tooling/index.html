Custom Bottle MOQ and Tooling: What It Actually Costs

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

Two numbers decide whether a custom bottle project is viable: the minimum order quantity and the tooling cost. Both are smaller than most buyers expect, provided the shape already exists.

A custom bottle does not have to mean a new mould. With 3,000+ moulds on the shelf, most briefs are already covered — the work is finding the closest one and changing decoration, not cutting steel.

Here is the honest arithmetic, including the cases where a new mould is the wrong answer.

When You Do Not Need a Mould

If your product fits a standard Boston round, a disc-top bottle or a straight-sided jar, the mould already exists somewhere in Shantou — possibly in our rack. Send a photo or a sample and we check before quoting tooling. This is the difference between a 25-day project and a 45-day one.

When a New Mould Is Worth It

A new mould makes sense when the shape is the brand — a distinctive silhouette, an unusual neck, a handle, a square that stacks better than anything stock. It stops making sense when the only change is colour or print: those are decoration, not tooling.

Mould ownership and exclusivity should be agreed in writing before the first payment. Ours is: the tooling is paid by the customer and reserved for that customer's use.

The Real Cost of a Small First Order

A 1,000 pc trial at 1,000 pcs MOQ is not the cheapest unit price you will ever see — it is the cheapest way to find out whether the pack survives your filling line. Buyers who skip it usually pay for the lesson in a rejected container.

The Three Things That Actually Drive the MOQ

Most buyers assume the MOQ is a policy. It is arithmetic: changeover time, scrap, and where the machine stops being the cost.

Changeover Dominates Below 1,000

Purging the machine, checking the shade against a standard, and scrapping the transition material takes the same time at 500 pcs as at 5,000. Below 1,000 the changeover is a third of the unit cost.

Decoration Has Its Own Floor

Silkscreen starts at 1,000 because the plate is a one-off. Spray coat, hot stamp and metallising start at 3,000 because the line set-up is heavier and the reject rate is higher.

Tubes and Pouches Are Different Businesses

Soft tubes start at 3,000 per design; printed pouches at 10,000, because each colour needs a rotogravure cylinder. The MOQ there is plates, not plastic.

Tooling: What You Own and What You Don't

A new blow mould is about USD 800 and 15-20 days. It belongs to the customer — we do not run a customer's mould for anyone else, and it can be shipped to another factory if you move.

Closure tooling is separate and quoted after the closure design is fixed. Injection moulds cost more than blow moulds because the tool is steel and the tolerance is tighter.

Questions We Get on This

Can I order less than 1,000 pcs?

On stock colours, sometimes yes — ask. On custom colour or print, 1,000 pcs is the practical minimum because of set-up time on the line.

Who owns the mould?

The customer who pays for it, and it is reserved for their use. This is agreed in writing before production.

Can you copy a competitor's bottle?

We can match a shape you own or have the right to use. We do not copy a protected design — if the shape is registered, we will say so.

How long from first email to shipped order?

Typically 45–60 days: 5 days for DFM and quotation, 25 days for T1 samples, 25–35 days for production.

Is the mould cost refundable?

No, but the mould belongs to you and can be transferred. Some factories offer a rebate after a volume threshold; we quote that case by case.

How long does tooling take?

15–20 days for a blow mould, then 7 days for a sample from it.

Can I use my existing mould?

Yes if it fits our machine platens — send the drawing and we check before quoting.
