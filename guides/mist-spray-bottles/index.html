Mist Spray Bottles: the Orifice Decides the Mist

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

A mist bottle is a bottle plus a sprayer, and the sprayer's orifice decides whether the product comes out as a fine mist or a wet sputter.

Mist sprayers are sold by output and orifice. The bottle is almost incidental — which is why a 20/410 neck exists.

Orifice and Output

0.10–0.15 ml per spray is the fine mist range. A larger orifice gives a wetter spray, which suits hair and cleaning but not toner.

Fine Mist

Cosmetic toner and setting spray; 20/410 or 24/410.

Coarse Mist

Hair and household; larger orifice, more output.

Testing With the Real Liquid

A sprayer set for water sprays a viscous toner unevenly. Test with the actual formula, at the actual temperature.

Where It Fails

A sprayer that drips after use, usually a worn valve or a formula that swells the gasket.

Questions We Get on This

What is a mist spray bottle used for?

Toner, setting spray, sanitizer, perfume samples and hair products.

What makes a fine mist?

A small orifice and a pre-compression or fine-mist valve, at 0.10–0.15 ml per spray.

Which neck?

20/410 for small bottles, 24/410 for 100 ml and above.

What is the MOQ?

1,000 pcs.
