60 ml Bottles: the Size Between Sample and Product

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

60 ml is two ounces: travel shampoo, hand sanitizer, serum refill and mouthwash. It is the size where a flip-top stops feeling like a sample.

60 ml is the practical travel size in Europe and the sample-plus size in the US, where it is sold as 2 oz.

Check it before you quote

The free Bottle Spec Checker tests whether your neck fits the closure and whether the wall survives a pump stroke.

Flip-Top or Disc-Top

At 60 ml a flip-top is comfortable in one hand; a disc-top is cheaper but doses loosely. Sanitizer usually takes a flip-top with a valve.

PET or HDPE

PET for clear gel and sanitizer; HDPE where the product is opaque or the bottle has to squeeze.

Where It Fails

A flip-top that does not click shut, and a bottle that is too stiff to squeeze at this size.

What to Send in a Brief

For a 60 ml pack used for travel shampoo and sanitizer, five answers settle it: capacity, neck, closure, quantity and whether the pack travels. Those decide the wall, the liner and the carton before a sample is made.

The Three Numbers That Matter

Capacity, neck and wall. Everything else — colour, decoration, carton — follows them.

What We Check Before Quoting

That the closure exists in that neck, that the wall survives the fill, and that the carton protects the shoulder.

What We Do Not Do

Filling, formulation and label design. We supply the container and the closure, tested together.

Common Mistakes at This Size

Two failures dominate returns at this size: a closure that leaks in transit, and a wall thin enough to crack the first time it is dropped.

Both are decided at the specification stage, not at the mould.
