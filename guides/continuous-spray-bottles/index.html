Continuous Spray Bottles: Pre-Compression, and Why They Cost Ten Times a Trigger

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

A continuous spray bottle holds pressure and delivers a fine, steady mist for a full second. It is the most expensive closure in the catalogue, and the reason is the pump.

A continuous sprayer pressurises the liquid with a few pumps, then releases a steady mist for about a second. No propellant, no aerosol can — just a different pump.

How It Works

Pumping charges a small chamber; pressing the nozzle releases the stored pressure through a fine orifice. The spray is even because the pressure is, not because the pump is.

Pre-Compression

The chamber fills to a set pressure before it releases; that is what makes the mist even.

Non-Aerosol

No propellant gas, so no aerosol regulations.

Why It Costs So Much

The valve and chamber are precision parts; the assembly is slower and the reject rate is higher. A continuous sprayer can cost several times a standard trigger.

Where It Pays

Sun care, hair styling, cleaning products where even coverage matters. Not for a product that is poured or pumped.

Questions We Get on This

What is a continuous spray bottle?

A bottle with a pre-compression pump that delivers a steady mist for about a second per press.

Is it an aerosol?

No — it uses a mechanical pump, not a propellant gas.

What is the MOQ?

3,000 pcs typically, because the valve assembly is volume-based.

What sizes?

200–300 ml is the common range.
