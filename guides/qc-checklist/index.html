Incoming QC: the Eleven Checks That Catch a Bad Batch

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

What to check when a container arrives, in the order that catches the most problems for the least time.

A container that fails at the filling line costs ten times what it costs to reject at the door. These are the checks we run, in the order we run them.

Paperwork First

Packing list against the PO, the declaration for your market, and the certificate pack. A missing declaration stops a container at customs more often than a bad bottle does.

Dimensional Checks

Weight per piece on a 0.01 g balance, wall at four points with a micrometer, neck with a go/no-go gauge, and height against the drawing.

On a multi-part pack, check the assembly: torque each thread, confirm the O-rings are present and seated, and vacuum-test one pack per carton sample.

Visual and Functional

Colour against the approved master under daylight, not office light. Then fill one with water, cap it, invert for an hour, and shake it.

What to Do With a Failure

Quarantine the pallet, photograph the failure against the specification, and send the batch number. A supplier can only fix what is documented.

Questions We Get on This

How many pieces should I inspect?

A sample per pallet for dimensions, plus a fill test on one pack per carton sample.

What is the most common failure?

Neck dimension, then weight variation between cavities.

Do I need a lab?

No. Scale, caliper, gauge and a torque meter cover most of it.
