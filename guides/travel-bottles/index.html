Travel Bottles: 50 ml Is the Airline Number, and the Closure Is the Leak

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

Travel sizes are decided by a rule, not by design: 100 ml per container in hand luggage. That makes 50 ml and 100 ml the only two sizes worth moulding — and the closure the only thing that matters.

Every airline regime converges on the same limit: containers of 100 ml or less, in a clear one-litre bag. So a travel range is 30, 50 and 100 ml — anything larger is not a travel bottle, it is a small bottle.

The bottle is easy. The closure decides whether the product arrives at the destination.

The Three Closures That Survive a Flight

Cabin pressure changes during a flight, and a bottle that sealed on the ground can weep in the air. Three closures handle it: a screw cap with a gasket, a flip-top with a tight spigot, and a disc-top with a silicone valve.

A plain flip-top with no gasket is the one that leaks into the washbag. It is also the cheapest, which is why travel sets leak.

Screw Cap With Gasket

The most reliable and the least convenient. Used for oils and thin liquids.

Flip-Top With a Spigot

A spigot that seats into the orifice holds pressure better than a flat flip lid. Check the spigot depth on the sample, not the drawing.

Disc-Top With a Valve

A silicone valve stops flow until squeezed, which is what a shampoo or lotion needs in a bag.

Why 50 ml Sells and 100 ml Is the Value Pack

50 ml is the unit people buy for a weekend; 100 ml is the maximum allowed and reads as better value. A travel set usually pairs both: 50 ml for the carry-on, 100 ml for a longer trip.

For a brand, the 50 ml is the sampler that gets a first trial, and the 100 ml is the one that gets rebought. That is why the same mould family runs both.

Where Travel Bottles Fail in Practice

Three failures, all testable before a container ships.

Leaking at the Neck Thread

A 24/415 bottle with a 24/410 cap threads on and weeps. Confirm the thread code.

The Label Comes Off in the Bag

Humidity plus abrasion. Synthetic label with a waterproof adhesive, or silkscreen.

The Bottle Cracks in a Suitcase

A 0.4 mm wall on a 100 ml survives a handbag, not a checked case. 0.5 mm is the practical floor.

What to Send in a Brief

Capacity, neck, closure type, and whether the pack will be sold as a set or singly. Sets need matched label positions across sizes, which is a decision made at artwork stage, not after.

Questions We Get on This

What is the maximum size for a travel bottle?

100 ml per container in hand luggage under most regimes; 50 ml is the common retail travel size.

Which closure leaks least in a flight?

A screw cap with a gasket, or a disc-top with a silicone valve. A plain flip-top is the usual culprit.

Can travel bottles be refilled?

Yes, and most are sold as refillable. Specify a durable thread and a valve cap.

What is the MOQ?

1,000 pcs per colour on stock moulds; sets with matched labels quote per component.
