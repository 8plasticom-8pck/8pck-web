Child-Resistant Caps: What Makes a Closure Child-Resistant

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 16 November 2026

A child-resistant cap is a tested system, not a cap: closure, bottle and liner together, on a specific neck thread, opened by two motions an adult finds easy and a child does not.

Child resistance is a performance standard, not a feature. A closure passes or fails as part of an assembled pack — the cap on that bottle, with that liner — and the same cap on a different neck may fail without any change to the cap.

That is why a CR pack cannot be specified from a catalogue photo. It is specified by the motion, the neck thread and the test, and all three have to be settled before the mould is cut.

Two Motions, Two Mechanisms

The push-and-turn cap has inner lugs that only engage the bottle thread when the cap is pressed down. The squeeze-and-turn cap has a skirt that has to be pinched on two sides before it will rotate. Both need a second, simultaneous action; that is the whole principle, and neither is meaningfully harder for an adult with normal hand strength.

What differs is the failure mode. Push-and-turn caps jam when they are over-torqued on the filling line, because the lugs bite into the thread. Squeeze-and-turn caps crack at the hinge of the squeeze tab when the cap is applied at an angle. Both failures look like 'the cap is stuck' to the customer.

The Bottle Is Half the System

A CR closure needs a matching neck: the thread form, the neck height and the land all matter, and the closure often needs a collar or a specific finish that a standard bottle does not have.

This is the expensive part of a late change. Moving from a screw cap to a CR cap on the same diameter is usually a new neck insert — tooling — not a new cap order. A brand that plans to sell into a market with a CR requirement should specify the CR neck from the first drawing.

Which Markets Require It

In the US, the Poison Prevention Packaging Act sets the requirement by product hazard and age group, and the test is a protocol with children and adults. In the EU, child resistance follows the hazard classification of the substance rather than the pack, and the standard for the closure is a separate type test.

The practical consequence is the same in both: the requirement attaches to what is in the bottle, so a pack that was fine with a screw cap can need a CR closure in one market and not another. Ask for the market in the brief, not after the artwork is approved.

What the Test Actually Measures

The test is not 'does it feel stiff'. It measures whether a defined proportion of children can open it within a set time, and whether adults can open and re-close it without difficulty. A closure that is too hard for an adult fails as surely as one that is too easy for a child.

In production we check two things per batch: the torque required to open, and that the liner still seals after the cap has been opened and closed several times. The second is the one that catches a collar that creeps, and it is why we test the assembled pack rather than shipping caps loose.

What to Send in a Brief

The product hazard and the market, the neck you already have (or the bottle you already fill), and the quantity. If you have a target neck, send a bottle — thread form is the detail that decides whether any CR cap will fit.

Ordering Closures Without Ordering the Wrong Ones

A closure is only as good as the bottle it is tested on. Two suppliers can each be within tolerance and still leak as a pair, so the test has to be on the filled pack.

Send the Bottle, Not the Drawing

Dimensions do not capture thread start or the sealing land.

Send the Product

A gasket that passes with water can swell in a week with a solvent.

Cost and Lead Time

Closures are quoted per 1,000 pcs and up, with a separate tooling cost only if the shape does not exist. Standard caps and pumps are stock items; anything moulded for you starts at 3,000–5,000 pcs.

Questions We Get on This

What is a child-resistant cap?

A closure that needs two simultaneous motions to open — push-and-turn, or squeeze-and-turn — tested as part of the assembled pack.

Do CR caps need a special bottle?

Usually yes. The neck thread, height and land are part of the system, so it is normally a new neck insert rather than a new cap on an old bottle.

Which markets require child-resistant packaging?

The US under the PPPA and the EU by hazard classification. The requirement follows the substance, not the pack, so it can differ by market.

What neck do CR caps use?

38-400 for pharma and chemical packs, 24/410 and 28/410 for smaller consumer packs.

Can you supply the cap and the bottle together?

Yes — and for a CR pack that is the only way to prove the system. We test opening torque and seal after repeated opening.

What is the MOQ?

1,000 pcs per colour on existing moulds.

Do you supply closures separately?

Yes, but they are tested on the bottle before they ship.

What if my bottle is from another supplier?

Send a sample; we test the closure on your bottle.

Can you match a colour?

Yes — closure and bottle can be colour-matched from a Pantone reference.
