Body and Hair Oil Bottles: the Oil Decides the Pack

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

Baby oil, beard oil, cuticle oil and massage oil are all oil, and oil finds every weak point in a pack: the gasket, the seal, the label adhesive and the pump.

Oil is the hardest common product to package because it creeps. It migrates through gaskets that hold water, it swells elastomers that survive alcohol, and it lifts adhesive that works on a shampoo bottle.

The five products below share that problem and differ in one thing: how the dose is delivered. Once the closure is right, the bottle is the easy part.

Why Oil Finds the Gasket

Mineral oil and many plant oils swell EPDM and natural rubber, and they creep through EPE foam. A closure that passes a water leak test can fail an oil test within days, because water is polar and oil is not: the same elastomer behaves differently against the two.

The fix is a gasket in the right material — silicone where the oil is aggressive, a PE disc where the product is thin — and a test with the actual oil, not water. This is the single most common reason an oil sample leaks after approval.

Dropper, Brush or Pump

Beard oil and serum at 30 ml use a dropper, but a rubber bulb in contact with oil perishes. A push-button dropper or a glass pipette with a PE collar lasts longer. Cuticle oil at 10–15 ml uses a brush, and the wiper is the specification: too tight and the brush arrives dry, too loose and it floods.

Massage oil at 500 ml and above uses a pump, and the pump seal has to be oil-compatible. A standard lotion pump with a nitrile seal will swell and stop priming within a few hundred strokes.

Glass or Plastic

Glass is the default for small premium oils because it does not interact with the product and it feels right in the hand. It also triples the freight per unit at the same volume and breaks in a parcel.

PET is the practical alternative for 100 ml and above: clear, cheap, and shatter-resistant. The limit is the oil itself — citrus oils in particular can stress-crack PET over months, so a compatibility sample is not optional.

Sizing, Honestly

30 ml is the beard-oil and serum size, and it is the size where the dropper costs more than the bottle. 50–60 ml is the gift and travel size. 100–200 ml is retail hair oil, and 250 ml plus is professional massage oil, usually with a pump.

The jump from 100 to 250 ml is where the pack stops being a bottle with a dropper and becomes a bottle with a pump. That is a different closure, a different neck and usually a different mould, so the size decision should be made with the closure in mind.

What Oil Does to a Wall and a Seal Over a Year

Oil slowly permeates HDPE. Not enough to leak in a week, but enough that after a year on a shelf a thin-wall bottle can show a greasy film on the outside and lose a measurable amount of product. For a 12-month shelf life, a 0.9 mm wall is safer than 0.6 mm on an oil pack.

The same mechanism attacks the seal. A silicone gasket resists oil far better than EPDM, which is why oil closures specify silicone even though it costs more. On a 30 ml beard oil the difference is a few cents; on a failed pallet it is the whole order.

Labels and the Oil Ring

Oil wicks under a label edge. A paper label on a massage oil bottle will lift at the corner within weeks, usually on the side where the hand grips. PP film with a permanent adhesive survives; paper does not.

The second detail is the bottle neck: oil left in the thread after pouring collects dust and darkens. A wiper insert or a disc-top with a lip keeps the neck cleaner, which matters on a product that sits in a bathroom for a year.

Questions We Get on This

What bottle is used for beard oil?

30 ml glass or PET with a dropper. A push-button dropper avoids the rubber bulb that perishes in oil.

Can baby oil go in a PET bottle?

Yes, with a compatibility sample. Mineral oil is generally benign in PET, but the gasket and the label adhesive are what fail first.

Why does my oil pump stop working?

The pump seal has swelled in oil. Use an oil-compatible seal, or move to a disc-top for viscous oils.

Does oil damage plastic bottles?

Some oils, especially citrus, stress-crack PET over months. Test the actual formula rather than a generic oil.

What size is a massage oil bottle?

500 ml to 1 L for professional use, 250 ml for retail. Above 500 ml a pump is standard.
