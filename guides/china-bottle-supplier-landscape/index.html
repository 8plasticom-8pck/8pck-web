The Chinese Bottle Supplier Landscape: Four Types, and How to Tell Which You Need

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

Suppliers in this industry are not one market. They are four, and a buyer who compares a catalog house with a single-factory desk on price alone will pick the wrong one. Here is the map, including where we sit on it.

Search for a plastic bottle supplier in China and you will get four kinds of company presented in the same uniform: a website, a MOQ line, a certificate strip. They are not interchangeable, and the differences show up three months into the relationship rather than on the quote sheet.

This page describes the four types, what each is genuinely good at, where each tends to fail, and how to tell them apart in a single call. It is published by 8PCK, which is one of the four — that disclosure is in the last section.

Type 1 · the Catalog House

Thousands of product pages, a dozen language versions, an enquiry form that routes to a sales team. They are the easiest to find and usually the fastest to reply, because the first reply is a catalog page rather than an answer.

What they are good at: breadth. If you need forty items across bottles, jars, pumps and tubes from one purchase order, a catalog house will assemble it. That is a real service, and it is why they dominate retail sourcing.

Where they fail: when the answer depends on a drawing. A catalog is a list of what exists; a question about wall thickness at the shoulder, or whether a 24/410 pump will survive a viscous formula, needs someone standing next to the machine. In a catalog house that person is two emails away, and the second email is often internal.

Type 2 · the Single-Factory Export Desk

One plant, one product range, an English-speaking desk in front of it. The range is narrower — often one or two processes — but the person answering has walked the floor.

What they are good at: consistency. The same team quotes, follows the trial and handles the complaint. Changes to a mould are decided by the people who cut it.

Where they fail: breadth, and sometimes responsiveness outside their range. If half your order is outside their process, they will either broker it (see Type 4) or decline.

Type 3 · the Full-Service Manufacturer

Mould shop, injection, blow moulding, decoration and sometimes filling under one roof, with an export department attached. This is the type that can carry a brand from drawing to a filled, labelled pack.

What they are good at: the projects nobody else will take — a new shape with an unusual closure, a four-part assembly, a decoration that needs three passes.

Where they fail: small orders. The overhead is real, the minimums are high, and a 2,000-piece trial will be quoted politely and deprioritised quietly. That is not bad faith; it is arithmetic.

Type 4 · the Trading Company

No plant. They hold relationships with several factories and add a margin for coordination, sometimes for QC, sometimes for nothing.

What they are good at: assembling a mixed container when no single factory can fill it, and absorbing the language and paperwork burden for a buyer who does not want it.

Where they fail: when something goes wrong. The factory hears the complaint through a third party, and the fix depends on who is paying for it. Ask to see the business licence early — a trader will present the factory's name as 'our partner', which is true but not the same as being the manufacturer.

How to Tell Them Apart in One Call

Ask which process makes the part, and listen for a machine name rather than a product name. Ask who signs the drawing and who owns the tool. Ask what they will not do — a factory with a narrow range refuses quickly and specifically; a broker rarely refuses anything.

Then ask for the certificate number, not the certificate image, and check it with the issuing body while you are still on the call. None of these questions is hostile; the answers take four minutes and decide whether the next three months are calm.

A Shortlist Method That Works

Take three suppliers, deliberately of different types, and give each the same six-field brief. Compare the questions they ask back, not the numbers they send. The supplier who asks about fill weight, decoration and destination is reading your brief; the one who sends a catalog has read your subject line.

Then ask each for one thing they would change in your specification. A factory that will not criticise a drawing is either not reading it or not able to change it.

Questions We Get on This

Are Chinese bottle suppliers all factories?

No. The four common types are catalog houses, single-factory export desks, full-service manufacturers and trading companies. Ask which process makes the part and who owns the mould to tell them apart.

Which type is best for a first order?

A single-factory export desk or a catalog house, depending on whether the brief is one item or forty. A full-service manufacturer is usually the wrong first call because minimums are high and small trials get deprioritised.

How do I verify a supplier is the manufacturer?

Ask for the unified social credit code and check it on gsxt.gov.cn, and ask for the name on the contract and the invoice to match. A trading company will name the factory as a partner.

Is this page independent?

No. It is published by 8PCK, which is described in it as a Type 2 supplier. The other three types are described by what they do, without ranking them.
