Recycled Plastic Bottles: What the Percentage Actually Means

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

rPET and PCR HDPE change the colour, the price and the paperwork — and the number on the artwork is the least reliable part of the claim.

A recycled bottle is not one product. rPET from collected bottles, PCR HDPE from collected milk jugs, industrial scrap from a factory's own reject stream, and bio-attributed resin all get called recycled, and they behave differently in the mould, in the colour and in the paperwork.

The percentage on the artwork is the easiest thing to print and the hardest thing to prove. What a buyer should ask for is not the number but the chain: which stream, which audit, which test report.

Why the Percentage Is the Wrong Question

Thirty percent rPET from a bottle-to-bottle stream and thirty percent from a mixed-stream flake are both 'thirty percent rPET'. The first can go into a food bottle with a challenge test behind it; the second cannot. The number does not distinguish them, and the price difference is real.

The question that does distinguish them: what is the input stream, and who audited it. A supplier who answers 'post-consumer bottles, EuCertPlast audited, food-grade approved' is selling something different from one who answers 'recycled material'.

What rPET Does to the Look of the Bottle

Below about 30 % rPET the tint is hard to see against virgin PET. Between 30 and 50 % a grey-green cast appears in thick sections; above 70 % it is visible on a shelf under shop light even in a thin wall.

For a clear bottle sold on transparency, this is the real constraint: the percentage the brand wants and the clarity the brand wants pull in opposite directions. The usual compromise is 30–50 % with a slightly blue masterbatch to neutralise the cast, and a tinted label to hide the rest.

PCR HDPE: Opaque, So Colour Is the Problem

PCR HDPE from milk jugs is naturally off-white to grey, and the variation batch to batch is larger than virgin. A white bottle at 100 % PCR is achievable but needs a heavier titanium dose, which raises cost and can affect the wall's impact strength.

For coloured packs the recycled content often disappears into the colour: a black or dark blue bottle at 50 % PCR is visually identical to virgin. That is why detergent and shampoo move to recycled content faster than premium skincare.

The Paperwork That Makes the Claim Defensible

A recycled-content claim needs three documents to survive a retailer's audit: a certificate of recycled content from the recyclate supplier, a chain-of-custody statement covering the conversion, and — for food contact — a challenge test on the recyclate under EU 10/2011, not just on the virgin polymer.

Under the PPWR the calculation method is still being finalised, and there is live debate about whether recyclate from outside the EU should count before import controls exist. A brand that prints a percentage today should keep the audit trail, because the method may change under it.

How the Claim Is Checked in Practice

The cheapest test of a recycled claim is a density check, not a document. rPET and virgin PET have the same density, so that tells you nothing; a UV-visible spectrum or a DSC scan shows the difference between a recycled and virgin melt, because the thermal history is never identical.

For a brand, the practical step is to ask the converter for the recyclate supplier's name and then verify that supplier's certification independently. A certificate without a supplier name is a PDF, not a claim.

What It Does to the Mould and the Cycle

Recycled resin melts slightly differently: rPET dries slower and holds moisture more stubbornly than virgin, so the drying time before moulding goes up and the risk of hydrolysis in the barrel goes with it. A bottle that passes with virgin PET can show splay or weak weld lines on rPET if the dryer is set to the virgin recipe.

Cycle time moves by 2-5 % rather than 20 %, but the reject rate on the first production run is where the money goes. Budget a second sample round when switching a running pack to recycled content — not because of the resin, but because every setting on the line was tuned to the old one.

The Honest Version of the Claim

Three sentences survive scrutiny, and they are the ones worth putting in a spec: the percentage by weight, the stream it comes from, and the audit behind it. A claim that says 'made with recycled plastic' and nothing else will be challenged by any retailer with a packaging team.

There is also a design consequence that brands usually miss: a bottle with a full-body sleeve or a metallised layer is hard to recycle, so the recycled content goes in the bin with the pack. Content and recyclability are two different claims, and a bottle can pass the first while failing the second.

Where Recycled Content Changes the Specification

Three places. Wall thickness: PCR HDPE has slightly lower impact strength, so a 0.55 mm wall may need 0.60 mm at the base. Colour: a masterbatch is now a specification item, not a decoration choice. And regrind policy: adding factory regrind on top of PCR can push the total recycled content above the claimed figure, which is fine legally but must be declared consistently.

None of these is a reason to avoid recycled content. They are the reasons it costs more than virgin and takes a sample round to get right.

Questions We Get on This

What is the difference between rPET and PCR?

rPET is recycled PET, usually from bottles. PCR means post-consumer recycled and can be PET, HDPE or PP. All rPET from bottles is PCR; not all PCR is PET.

Can a 100 % rPET bottle be food grade?

Yes, if the recyclate is food-grade approved and a challenge test under EU 10/2011 has been done on it. The percentage alone says nothing about food contact.

Does rPET look different from virgin PET?

Below 30 % the difference is hard to see. At 50 % a grey-green cast appears in thick sections; at 100 % it is visible on shelf.

Is recycled content more expensive?

Usually 5–20 % more per unit at 30–50 %, and more at 100 %, because food-grade recyclate costs more than virgin resin and colour matching takes an extra sample round.

Do I need a certificate for a recycled claim?

Yes — a recycled content certificate from the recyclate supplier plus a chain-of-custody statement. For food contact, a challenge test on the recyclate itself.
