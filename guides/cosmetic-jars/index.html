Cosmetic Jars: What 30 g Actually Means, and Why Wall Thickness Sells

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

A “30 g jar” is named after the fill weight, not the volume — which is why two 30 g jars can look completely different on shelf. This page covers fill weight, single versus double wall, and how the cap decides the feel.

Cosmetic jars are sold by fill weight: 30 g, 50 g, 100 g. The number tells you how much product goes in, not how big the jar is. A 30 g jar in single-wall PET and a 30 g jar in double-wall PETG hold the same amount and read as two different price points.

That gap — same fill, different perceived value — is the whole game in skincare packaging.

Single Wall vs Double Wall

A single-wall jar is one moulded piece: lighter, cheaper, and the standard for volume skincare. A double-wall jar has an outer shell and a separate inner cup, so the product never touches the decorated surface and the wall can be thick without using more resin than necessary.

Double wall is what makes a jar feel expensive. It is also what makes it heavier to ship, and the two parts have to be assembled — both of which show up in the unit price.

Why the Cap Decides the Feel

Buyers judge a jar by the lid: the sound it makes and the weight in the hand. A thin PP cap on a thick-wall jar feels wrong immediately. For premium lines the cap is often the heavier part — ABS or thick PP, sometimes with a metal-look insert.

If you are sampling, judge the jar with its cap on. Half the perceived quality is in the closure.

Threads, Refills and the EU Refill Push

A screw jar can be refilled only if the refill cup is designed for it; most double-wall jars are not. If refillability is part of the claim, say so at the brief stage — it changes both the mould and the packaging configuration.

Where Cosmetic Jars Leak, and It Is Usually the Liner

A cream jar that leaks in transit is rarely a mould problem. Three causes cover most of it.

Liner Incompatible With the Formula

Oil-based creams swell EPE liners; silicone liners cost more and resist. If the formula has a high oil phase, specify the liner with the jar.

Over-Torque at the Filling Line

A torque above the jar's spec distorts the liner and opens a gap on one side. Specify a torque range with the capping machine, not a 'tight' instruction.

Thread Depth on a Double-Wall Jar

Double-wall jars have an inner cup; the thread is on the outer shell. A liner that fits a single-wall jar may not sit flat on a double-wall rim.

Frosting, Spray Coat and What They Cost

Frosting is a surface treatment from 1,000 pcs and reads as soft-touch glass. Spray coat and metallising start at 3,000 and add 5-8 days. For a 30 g cream at launch volume, frosting plus label is usually the cheapest premium look.

Questions We Get on This

Is a 30 g jar the same as a 30 ml jar?

Not exactly. Grams measure fill weight, millilitres measure volume. A 30 g cream at typical density is close to 30 ml, but a light emulsion can be 35 ml in the same jar.

Can you print on a jar lid?

Yes — silkscreen or hot stamp on the lid top, at 1,000 and 3,000 pcs MOQ respectively.

What is the MOQ for a frosted jar?

1,000 pcs. Frosting is a surface treatment, not a colour, so it does not add a colour changeover.

Can the jar be made from recycled material?

PET and PETG can include recycled content; the percentage changes clarity and needs its own declaration. Ask for the grade before you promise a recycled claim.

What is a standard cosmetic jar size?

30 g, 50 g and 100 g by fill weight; 15 ml for samples and travel.

Do you supply the cap and liner?

Yes — screw lids with EPE, silicone or induction liners, tested on the jar.

Can the jar be frosted?

Yes, from 1,000 pcs. Frosting does not add a colour changeover.
