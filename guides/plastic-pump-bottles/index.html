Plastic Pump Bottles: Sizes, Necks and What to Specify

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou · Updated 26 October 2026

A pump bottle is three parts sold as one product — bottle, neck finish and pump. Get the neck wrong and the pump leaks; get the wall thickness wrong and the bottle collapses under a full stroke. This guide covers the sizes we mould, the necks they take, the pumps that fit, and the four numbers that settle a brief.

Pump bottles are the default pack for anything dispensed by the stroke: hand soap, body wash, shampoo, lotion, sanitiser, pet shampoo. The category looks simple and is not, because the bottle and the pump are made by different processes and still have to fit together at speed on a filling line.

We blow the bottle and injection-mould the pump and cap in the same building. That matters more than it sounds: a pump that fits our bottle is tested on our bottle, not on a drawing.

The Three Parts, and Which One Actually Fails

Almost every complaint about a pump bottle traces to one of three interfaces: the pump against the neck, the dip tube against the base, or the shoulder against the stroke. Each has a different fix.

The Bottle

The bottle provides stiffness, not strength. A 500 ml PET at 28 g holds its shape under a full stroke; the same mould run at 22 g flexes at the shoulder and draws air back through the pump.

This is why weight is quoted with the mould rather than negotiated after the sample: drop below the wall the mould was cut for and the pump starts to gulp.

The Neck

A 24/410 pump on a 24/415 bottle threads on and leaks. The difference is 2.5 mm of thread height, which is invisible on a photo and obvious on a filling line.

If you already buy closures, send one. We test it on the bottle before quoting rather than assuming the neck is standard — it usually is, and occasionally it is not.

The Pump

Three failure modes dominate: a spring that corrodes with a high-salt formula, a valve that clogs with a particulate gel, and an actuator too small for the viscosity, which feels stiff after the first hundred strokes.

All three are testable in a week. None are fixable after the container ships.

Sizes and Which Markets Buy Them

Nominal capacity is measured to the shoulder. With the pump inserted, usable fill is typically 5–8 % lower — a 500 ml bottle holds about 470–490 ml. Check the label claim against usable volume, not nominal.

Why 500 ml Dominates in English-Speaking Markets

It is a shelf-price decision rather than a technical one: 500 ml hits the price point that reads as value without looking like a bulk pack. 250–300 ml performs the same role in Germany and the Nordics.

When 750 ml Pays

Only with a 28/410 pump and a wall of at least 0.65 mm on the shoulder. Below that, the bottle flexes and the pump draws air for the last fifth of the fill.

Wall Thickness, Weight and Where the Cost Sits

Resin is bought by the tonne, so weight is the first cost lever and the last one most brands look at. A 500 ml PET at 28 g and one at 36 g look identical on a shelf and differ by roughly 25 % in material cost.

The trap is cutting weight without re-testing the pump. Two grams off the shoulder is invisible; it is also enough to make a 2.0 ml stroke feel spongy.

What the Price Is Made Of

Material (40–55 % of the bottle), machine time (15–25 %), decoration (variable), packing and inspection (5–10 %). The pump is quoted separately and can be 40–60 % of the delivered unit price on small bottles.

Where Weight Can Safely Come Off

The body wall tolerates more reduction than the shoulder or the base. If a brand needs a lighter bottle for freight, we re-quote with a thinner body and the same shoulder — and re-test the pump.

Testing Before the Container Ships

Every order runs the same three checks on a filled sample before packing. They take a day and prevent the two complaints that cost the most: leaking in transit and a pump that stops dosing halfway.

The 20-Stroke Test

Fill, fit, and pump twenty times. Watch for a slow return on the actuator (valve too tight for the viscosity) and for product escaping at the collar (thread mismatch or a missing gasket).

The Standing and Drop Test

A filled bottle must stand flat with the dip tube cut to the base curve, not pressed against it. Dropped from 1.2 m onto a corner, the shoulder should crack before the neck — if the neck goes, the mould is too thin there.

The Compatibility Soak

For alcohol or essential-oil formulas, a filled sample sits four weeks before approval. PET can craze at the shoulder in that window; better to find it in the sample than in a container.

What to Send in a Brief

Four numbers and one closure: capacity, neck finish, material, quantity, and the pump you intend to use (or ask us to match one). With those we quote unit price, packing data and lead time together, so the freight calculation is yours to check rather than a guess.

If the shape is new, we check the 3,000+ moulds first; a blow mould is about USD 800 and 15–20 days.

Questions We Get on This

What is the most common pump bottle size?

500 ml in the US, UK and Australia; 250–300 ml in Germany and the Nordics; 100 ml for travel and hotel.

What is the difference between 24/410 and 28/410?

The neck diameter: 24 mm and 28 mm, both with a 410 thread. A 28/410 pump will not fit a 24/410 bottle, and the bottle diameter changes with it.

Can I use a foaming pump on a standard bottle?

Yes if the neck matches and the formula is thin enough. A foaming pump needs a low-viscosity soap and a foaming agent; a thick gel will not foam.

How much does a 500 ml pump bottle weigh?

Our 500 ml PET is 28 g ± 1 g. HDPE equivalents run 25–35 g depending on wall, and a 750 ml is 40–55 g.

What wall thickness does a 750 ml bottle need?

At least 0.65 mm on the shoulder for a 28/410 pump. Below that the bottle flexes under a full stroke.

Do you supply the pump with the bottle?

Yes — lotion, foaming and mist pumps in 20/410, 24/410 and 28/410, fitted and tested on the bottle before packing.

What is the MOQ?

1,000 pcs per colour on an existing mould; a new shape is about USD 800 and 15–20 days for tooling.

Is a 500 ml bottle really 500 ml?

Nominal capacity is to the shoulder. With the pump fitted, usable fill is about 470–490 ml.
