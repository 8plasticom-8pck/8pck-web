    How it is calculated

    The Formula, the Assumptions, and Where It Stops

    1. Side wall = π × D × H × t.
    2. Base = π × (D/2)² × t × 1.6 (the base is thicker than the wall).
    3. Shoulder = π × D × min(D/2, 30) × t × 0.8.
    4. Mass = total volume × density (PET 1.34, HDPE 0.95, PP 0.90, PETG 1.27 g/cm³).

    Assumptions. A smooth body with no ribs, handle or thick collar; those add 5–15 %, which is why the result is quoted ±10 %.

    Standards we work to. ISO 9001:2015 (quality system, cert PCC85724Q01277R1S) · EU 10/2011 (plastic food-contact, migration report E2032) · RoHS (PE B-R01612E2033, PET B-R201133464) · ISO 4180 (packaging for transport). The calculator is a planning tool: the approved drawing and the migration report decide the final specification.

    Where it does not apply. It is an estimate for planning. The real weight comes from the drawing and a trial mould.

    Why the Weight Matters More Than the Wall

    Resin is priced by the kilogram, so a 2 g difference on a 28 g bottle is 7 % of the material cost — and on a 40,000-piece container it is 80 kg of resin. Weight is also what the container payload and the pallet limits are calculated from.

    What This Estimate Cannot See

    Neck finish, handle, ribs and any thick section at the base. Those add 5–15 %, so treat the number as a planning figure and confirm against a sample.

    Questions About This Tool

    How much does a 500 ml PET bottle weigh?

    Around 25–30 g at 0.55 mm wall; heavier with a pump collar or thick base.

    How does rPET change the weight?

    Not at all — same density; only the resin cost and colour change.

    Is thinner always cheaper?

    Cheaper in resin, more expensive in returns: below 0.5 mm the shoulder flexes and the pump draws air.
