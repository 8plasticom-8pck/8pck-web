    How it is calculated

    The Formula, the Assumptions, and Where It Stops

    1. Total product = dose × uses per day × days of supply.
    2. Take the smallest stock bottle above that total.
    3. Annual volume = units × capacity — the number that decides whether a new mould is justified.

    Assumptions. Pump output is constant within tolerance; a viscous product delivers slightly less per stroke than water.

    Standards we work to. ISO 9001:2015 (quality system, cert PCC85724Q01277R1S) · EU 10/2011 (plastic food-contact, migration report E2032) · RoHS (PE B-R01612E2033, PET B-R201133464) · ISO 4180 (packaging for transport). The calculator is a planning tool: the approved drawing and the migration report decide the final specification.

    Where it does not apply. Dose is a label claim. If the label says 0.3 ml, the pump must be specified and tested to deliver 0.3 ml.

    Dose Decides the Bottle, Not the Brief

    A 1.2 ml pump twice a day for 60 days needs 144 ml, so the pack is 150 ml — not the 100 ml a brand usually starts with. This is the single most common sizing error in skincare.

    Why Annual Volume Matters

    Units × capacity sets the resin order and whether a new mould is justified. Below 20,000 units a year, a stock shape is almost always the right answer.

    Questions About This Tool

    What dose should I use?

    Face serum 0.5–1.2 ml; body lotion 2.0 ml; cleanser 1.0–1.5 ml. Ask the pump supplier for the stroke.

    Does the pump output stay constant?

    Within tolerance; a viscous product delivers less per stroke than water.

    What if the result is between sizes?

    Take the larger size — an under-filled pack reads short on shelf.
