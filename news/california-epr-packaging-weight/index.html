California EPR Fees Are Landing on Packaging Weight. What It Changes in a Bottle Spec

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou

Plastics News reports that plastics face high fees under California's EPR rules, with exemptions possible for recyclable formats. For a bottle buyer, the fee is a function of material and grams — both are in your control.

What Changed

California's extended producer responsibility programme is moving from framework to fees. Plastics News reports that plastic packaging faces comparatively high fees, with the possibility of relief for formats that are recyclable or otherwise easier to process.

What It Means for Buyers

For anyone buying bottles, that is not a policy story. It is a specification story. EPR fees are calculated on material type and weight, so the cheapest bottle to buy is not always the cheapest to place on a California shelf.

Three numbers in your spec start to matter more than they did last year. The first is gram weight: a 500 ml PET bottle at 28 g and one at 22 g look identical and are priced differently by an EPR scheme. The second is material mix — a PET bottle with a PP cap and a full-body PVC sleeve is harder to recycle than the same bottle with a separable cap and a paper label. The third is recycled content, which some schemes reward.

What to Ask a Supplier

What that means in practice: ask for the bottle weight in grams, the resin, the closure material and whether the label or sleeve can be removed. Then ask what the same shape weighs in a thinner wall — on a 500 ml PET pump bottle, dropping from 0.55 mm to 0.45 mm saves several grams per unit, provided the pump still seats without flexing.

None of this requires a new mould. It requires a supplier who will quote weight, not just price — and who will tell you when a thinner wall is a bad idea.

What It Changes in a Bottle Spec

Weight Becomes a Recurring Cost

Under EPR, a heavier bottle costs more every year, not once. A 500 ml at 28 g instead of 36 g is an 8 g saving multiplied by every unit sold and every year of the fee schedule.

Where Weight Can Come Off Safely

The body wall, not the shoulder or base. Removing grammage from the shoulder is what makes a pump draw air.
