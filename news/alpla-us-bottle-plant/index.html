Alpla Is Building a Bottle Plant in the US. What It Changes for Buyers Who Import

By Joe Joo · 8PCK · Shantou

Recycling Today reports Alpla adding US bottle capacity. For a buyer importing from Asia, the question is which part of the order the local plant makes cheaper — and which part it does not.

What Changed

Recycling Today reports Alpla is building a plastic bottle plant in the United States. Alpla already moulds in North America, so the news is about capacity and proximity rather than a new entrant.

What It Means for Buyers

For an importer, local capacity changes one number: freight. A 500 ml bottle at 28 g fills a container with air, so ocean freight is a large share of landed cost at low unit values. A plant in the same country removes that share and shortens the reorder cycle from 35 days to days.

What it does not change is tooling and MOQ. A local plant still needs a mould for a shape it does not own, and still wants a minimum run to justify a changeover. That is why buyers split orders: standard shapes locally, custom shapes where the mould already exists.

What to Ask a Supplier

The second effect is on lead time expectations. Once a buyer has a domestic source at 10 days, the imported alternative has to justify 25–35 days with price, decoration or a shape nobody local stocks.

For a Chinese exporter the practical answer is not to compete on speed for commodity shapes. It is to own the mould: the shape, the finish, the closure combination that a local plant would have to tool from scratch.

Source: Recycling Today
