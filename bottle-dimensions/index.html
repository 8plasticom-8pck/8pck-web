Lids Add Height

Retail packs are measured with the closure fitted, and the closure is where the last 20–60 mm comes from. A 500 ml bottle is 170 mm bare (168 mm on our own mould) and 192 mm with a disc-top, which is the difference between four rows per carton and five.

How These Numbers Were Built

  • Numbers come from a wall-thickness model — straight cylinder, shoulder 0.45 × diameter, 18 mm neck — calibrated on our own 500 ml PET mould (Ø73 × 168 mm, 28 g). They are typical, not a specification.
  • Height is the bottle without a closure. Add the closure height from the second table to get the packed height.
  • Empty weight assumes a 0.50 mm PET body or a 0.80 mm HDPE body. A heavier or thicker bottle is a different number.
  • Full weight is empty weight plus 1.0 g per ml of water-like product. A 500 ml shampoo at 1.05 g/ml weighs about 553 g.
  • Tolerance on height and diameter is ±1.5 mm in production. The drawing for your mould governs; this table is for planning cartons, shelf fit and freight before a drawing exists.

What the Table Is Good For

Cartons. Packed height is bottle height plus closure. A 40 mm difference per bottle across 12 bottles is 480 mm of carton height — usually one more carton layer per pallet, or one less.

Freight. Empty weight drives air freight and sample courier; full weight drives ocean freight. At 2,000 ml the pack is 70 g empty and 2.1 kg full, and the container hits its weight limit long before its volume.

Shelf fit. Body diameter, not capacity, decides whether the bottle stands in the slot the retailer planned. A 73 mm bottle does not fit a 70 mm facing whatever the label says.

Fill weight. Full weight is what the co-packer's checkweigher sees. If the declared net content is 500 ml and the empty bottle varies by 2 g, the reject band has to absorb it.
